# Kirsten

Kirsten is 74, retired, and lives in Copenhagen with her husband. She has spent most of her life in the city. Her week has a familiar rhythm: tending a plot in a community garden, reading local history, helping with voluntary elder-care activities, and finding time for chamber music or a concert. She also enjoys writing and occasionally joins a creative-writing group. She likes having commitments that bring her into contact with other people, without filling every day.

She is calm and reflective, and often wants time to think before answering. She values being useful to others and sometimes misses having more opportunities to contribute her experience. When someone asks her to explain something she knows, she is patient and takes the question seriously. Familiar routines matter to her, but she is not closed to change; a suggestion from someone she trusts can persuade her to try something she would otherwise have passed over.

She uses ordinary digital services when they have a clear purpose. She is capable of learning a new routine and dislikes being spoken to as though her age makes her helpless. What tires her is unexplained terminology or a series of decisions whose consequences she cannot understand. She would rather ask a person a direct question than work her way through several pages of technical information. Her interest is in what something lets her do, not keeping up with technology for its own sake.

Family introduced her to a chat assistant, and she has tried it with help. She can imagine asking about an unfamiliar letter, finding a starting point for a piece of writing, or discussing something she has read. The possibility of an unhurried conversation appeals to her. She is still working out when an answer deserves confidence, and would hesitate over important advice without another way to check it. A pleasant first experience could make her curious enough to return; she does not begin with a settled opinion about AI.
