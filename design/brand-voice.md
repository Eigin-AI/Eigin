# Brand voice

How Eigin talks. This is the standard for copy across the product: marketing,
onboarding, settings, empty states, errors, anywhere the brand or the
application speaks to a person. It is not strictly marketing copy, but it
covers it.

## The brand and the agent

The brand and the agent speak differently. This document is about how *we* write copy. 

The agent, the AI you chat with, is its own voice. It carries a short set of behavioural guidelines baked into its system prompt that steer how it talks without spelling all of this out. Those guidelines are a distilled subset of the fundamentals below, adapted to live conversation: clarity, transparency, warmth, and a quiet confidence. The agent does not do vision; that one is ours.

When the two ever disagree, this document is the source of truth for copy and
the system prompt is the source of truth for the agent.

## Five fundamentals

### Warmth
Psychological safety. Respect the user's autonomy and acknowledge their
perspective. It matters because people trust Eigin when they feel in control
and understood. Traits: empathetic, human, never condescending, never overly
casual.

### Clarity
Precision without complexity. Every word serves a purpose. It matters because
no one should have to work to understand what we said. Traits: short
sentences, concrete language, active voice, no jargon.

### Confidence
Conviction in the product's purpose and the user's agency. It matters because
it earns trust in the product's reliability and in the user's own judgment.
Traits: declarative statements, honesty about limitations, no hedging. Say
plainly what we know and what we don't.

### Vision
A future state grounded in the product's reality, not hype. It matters
because it shows people where Eigin is headed. Traits: future-oriented,
inclusive ("we"), quietly bold, and always tied to mechanics. The mechanics
are the evidence that we deliver on the vision.

### Transparency
State things plainly and truly: what is, what isn't, and why. It matters
because trust depends on us not obfuscating. Traits: honest, specific, no
fine print.

## When they conflict

Priority order: **clarity and transparency** come first, then **warmth**, then
**confidence**, then **vision**.

Tone is always human, always direct, always respectful. We don't sound like a
corporation or a robot. When in doubt, test it: does this sound like something
a human would say to another human?

## Do's and don'ts

### Warmth

**Acknowledge the user's world before the product.** Trust comes from showing
you understand their situation, not from leading with features.
- Good: "You want to use cloud AI, but signing up for providers and getting
  API keys is a hassle."
- Bad: "Relay lets you use cloud AI easily."

**Use "you" and "your." Reinforce the user's control.**
- Good: "You own the memory. Nothing leaves unless you decide it does."
- Bad: "The memory is owned by the user."

**Respect the user's intelligence. Don't over-explain or talk down.**
- Good: "Local models are improving, but they're not yet a full replacement."
- Bad: "Local models are getting better, but they're still kind of basic."

### Clarity

**Use short, direct sentences.**
- Good: "Relay lets you buy credits in the app. Tap and go."
- Bad: "With Relay, users can purchase inference credits directly within the
  application, allowing for a seamless and frictionless experience."

**Use concrete language.**
- Good: "Models with zero data retention."
- Bad: "Models that don't retain data."

**Avoid jargon.**
- Good: "We aggregate requests so providers don't see your IP."
- Bad: "We use request batching to obfuscate your IP from providers."

### Confidence

**Make declarative statements. Don't hedge.**
- Good: "Eigin runs on your device. Nothing leaves unless you decide it does."
- Bad: "Eigin might run on your device and it probably won't share your data."

**Be transparent about limitations. Don't downplay trade-offs.**
- Good: "Local models are still getting there, so we support both local and
  cloud."
- Bad: "Local models are almost as good as cloud."

**Own the product's purpose. No false humility.**
- Good: "This isn't about profit. It's about making cloud AI simple and
  private."
- Bad: "We're just a small team, but we're trying our best."

### Vision

**Tie the future to the product's mechanics.**
- Good: "This is how cloud AI should work: private, simple, and under your
  control."
- Bad: "We are revolutionizing AI."

**Use inclusive language.**
- Good: "We're building a future where AI works for you, not platforms."
- Bad: "The future of AI is here."

**Be quietly bold. No hype.**
- Good: "Own your AI, starting now."
- Bad: "The most revolutionary AI experience ever."

### Transparency

**State costs, limitations, and trade-offs upfront.**
- Good: "Credits cost what the provider charges, plus Apple's 30% fee and a 5%
  fee for us to keep Relay running."
- Bad: "Credits are sold at a fair price."

**Explain the why.**
- Good: "We add a 5% fee to keep Relay running."
- Bad: "There is a small fee."

**Be direct about what the product does and doesn't do.**
- Good: "The code is open. Audit it. See for yourself that Relay only routes
  your requests and counts your credits."
- Bad: "Relay is totally private and secure."

## Anti-patterns

**Repetitive lists.**
- Bad: "No accounts, no keys, no friction."
- Good: "No accounts, no keys, just tap and go."

**Vague or passive phrasing.**
- Bad: "This isn't a profit driver."
- Good: "We're not doing this for profit."

**Empty triads.**
- Bad: "Private. Simple. Yours."
- Good: "Private, simple, and under your control."

**"It's not X, it's Y" constructions.**
- Bad: "It's not about profit, it's about making cloud AI easy."
- Good: "We're not doing this for profit, but to make cloud AI easy."

**Over-explaining or hedging.**
- Bad: "Local models are kind of getting better but they're not quite there
  yet."
- Good: "Local models are improving, but they're not a full replacement yet."
